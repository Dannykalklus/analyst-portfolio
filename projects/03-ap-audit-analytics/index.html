Findings and recommended follow-up

1. Missing department coding on 54.1% of payment lines

$6.07bn of payments cannot be linked to a department in the published data. Procedure: confirm whether the field is missing at source or only in the public extract, and test whether direct vouchers carry budget-owner approval.

2. 832 possible duplicate payments ($146.6m)

5 of the ten largest pairs were paid on the same day under different voucher numbers. Procedure: vouch both payments to separate invoices or obligations; check vendor statements for credits.

3. 1,056 fully identical payment lines ($371.0m)

Rows where voucher, amount, date, vendor and contract all match. These are usually split lines on one voucher, but they should reconcile to voucher totals. Procedure: agree a sample of vouchers to the general ledger.

4. Fixed-amount payments distort the digit profile

The first-digit test conforms, but standard amounts ($50, $500, $775, $1,550) create large two-digit spikes. Procedure: obtain the approved rate schedules and test that each fixed payment type matches one.

5. High concentration in a few counterparties

129 vendors take 80% of spending. Procedure: focus substantive testing on these vendors; confirm balances directly with pension funds and banks.
